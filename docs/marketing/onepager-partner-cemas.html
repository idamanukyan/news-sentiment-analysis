The gap

You're doing continuous monitoring with manual tools

Disinformation analysts spend their time on work a system should carry: watching the same channels every day, re-finding narratives across languages, reconstructing who published what and when, and rebuilding the evidence trail for every report. There's rarely a single continuous picture that spans Russian- and German-language sources at once — and when a narrative looks coordinated, proving it means stitching timestamps together by hand.

AIIM is built to be the layer underneath that work: it does the watching and the first-pass clustering, and hands the analyst a structured, auditable starting point instead of a blank page.

Methodology, in the open

How coordination is flagged

The point of a research tool is that the method is inspectable. Coordination scoring is transparent and contestable.

Coordination signal — pipeline
1

Cluster into narratives

Cross-lingual articles/posts grouped by narrative via keyword + AI relevance scoring.

2

Measure timing & content similarity

Within a narrative, look for near-simultaneous publication and near-identical framing across independent sources over a defined window.

3

Score & surface

Combine into a coordination score (timing / content / both), record the sources and articles involved, and raise an event for analyst review.

4

Analyst adjudicates

Review, confirm or dismiss — the human stays in the loop; the tool never auto-publishes a verdict.
