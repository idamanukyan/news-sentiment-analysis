The problem

The manipulation is coordinated. The response usually isn't.

Foreign information manipulation and interference — FIMI — is now a standing feature of the European information environment, not an election-day event. Kremlin-aligned narratives move across Russian-language media, Telegram channels and social platforms faster than any newsroom or oversight body can read them, seeding stories on energy, migration, Ukraine and NATO that surface in German-language discourse days later.

The people mandated to watch this — foundations, watchdogs, media authorities, DSA researchers — are mostly doing it by hand: monitoring channels manually, tracking narratives in spreadsheets, and writing reports from scratch. There is no continuous, cross-lingual picture of which narratives are spreading, whether the spread is coordinated, and how serious it is.

AIIM was built to close exactly that gap — and it was built against the hardest version of the problem: the Russian-language information space in a contested election.

What it does

From raw feeds to a defensible threat picture

Every capability maps to the vocabulary European oversight already uses — FIMI, coordinated inauthentic behaviour, and Digital Services Act reporting.

Narrative tracking

Detect & follow narratives

Keyword and AI relevance scoring surface emerging narratives across languages and cluster the articles carrying them — with an approval workflow so analysts stay in control of what counts.

Coordination

Flag coordinated campaigns

Cross-source detection identifies when the same narrative is pushed in lockstep — the coordinated-behaviour signal that distinguishes an organic story from an operation.

Alerting

Real-time analyst alerts

Custom rules, threat levels, assignment and resolution workflow, and live notifications — so a spike becomes a triaged case, not a missed signal.

Reporting

DSA-format reporting

Daily, weekly, incident and EU DSA-compliance reports exported to PDF, Excel, CSV and Markdown — the evidence layer oversight and funders actually ask for.

Case study — proof of deployment

Built and run against the Armenia 2026 election environment

AIIM was developed and deployed to monitor the information space around a live, contested national election — the most demanding test a system like this can face. It ingested and analysed sources continuously across Russian, Armenian and English, the exact multilingual, Kremlin-adjacent conditions European monitors care about.

  • Continuous multilingual ingestion from news, Telegram and social sources
  • AI narrative detection, coordination flagging and threat-level triage in production
  • Multi-organisation, role-based access with a full analyst workflow
  • DSA-format compliance reporting generated end-to-end

The Armenian deployment is the proof, not the product. The same engine points at any information space — and Russian-language capability transfers directly to the German market's number-one concern.
